2Ground clearance: the one spec people underrate

On broken roads, monsoon water, and big speed breakers, every millimetre counts. Below ~170 mm a loaded sedan starts scraping; above ~190 mm you stop worrying.

A loaded car loses 15–25 mm of effective clearance. ● 195 mm+ relaxed · ● 180–194 okay · ● under 180 careful.

3The real cost: 10 years, not the sticker

Over 200,000 km, fuel and depreciation dwarf the purchase price. A thirsty car with weak resale can cost ₹3–4 lakh more to own than a pricier-but-efficient one that holds value.

Lower is cheaper to own. Includes purchase + fuel + service + tyres/insurance − resale. Treat as a ±₹1.5 L band.

4How to actually decide

🛣️ Mostly smooth roads?

Prioritise mileage, low price, 5-star safety, boot space. Sedans and light crossovers win: Dzire, Amaze, Fronx, Aura.

⛰️ Rough roads or loaded trips?

Prioritise ground clearance, suspension, resale. Compact SUVs win: Brezza, Nexon, XUV 3XO, Victoris.

The one question that decides it: what are the worst roads you'll drive regularly (or on that once-a-year trip)? Optimise for those, not the highway you're on most days. And remember a wide, tall SUV is harder in tight city lanes and parking, so width matters too.

5Buyer questions, answered plainly

What does "on-road price" actually include?

Ex-showroom is just the start. On-road = ex-showroom + insurance (1st year, recurs yearly) + RTO road tax (~10–12% in Maharashtra) + registration + FASTag + optional extended warranty and accessories. Budget ₹1.5–2 lakh over ex-showroom on a ₹8 L car.

What is the CSD scheme — can I use it?

CSD (Canteen Stores Department) is the defence forces' subsidised purchase scheme — serving and ex-servicemen get part of the GST/cess waived, so ex-showroom is ~10–15% lower. If you're not eligible, your real price is higher than shown here; use these figures for comparison, not as your quote.

Is the extended warranty worth it?

Standard warranty is usually 3 years / 1,00,000 km. The extension (~₹16–21k) covers years 4–5. Worth it if you keep the car long — one electronic-module failure (infotainment, ABS/ESP) in year 4–5 can cost ₹25–60k. Skip it only if you sell within 3 years. It covers breakdowns, not wear items (brakes, tyres) or accidents.

Turbo or naturally-aspirated (NA)?

Turbo (Nexon, Kylaq, XUV 3XO) gives more punch but adds complexity and can cost more to maintain past warranty. NA (most Maruti, Honda) is simpler, proven, cheaper to run, with less outright grunt. For a long-keep family car, NA is the safer bet.

Do seat covers affect side airbags?

Yes. If your car has seat-mounted side airbags (most 6-airbag cars do), the airbag deploys through a seam on the outer front-seat edge. A cheap tight aftermarket cover can block it. Use the genuine cover, or insist on an "airbag-compatible" one with the tear-seam.

Do I need the handbrake to start on a hill?

Most modern cars have Hill Hold Assist. Stop on the incline, release the brake, and the car holds itself ~2 seconds while you move to the accelerator — no roll-back, no handbrake needed. The handbrake method still works for very steep slopes, but it's optional when Hill Hold is fitted.

Why does resale value matter so much?

Over 7–10 years, depreciation is often the single biggest cost — bigger than fuel. A car holding 70% of value (Maruti) vs 40% can be ₹2–3 lakh cheaper to own despite a higher sticker. That's why a popular car can be the cheaper long-term choice.
