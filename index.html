2. Ground clearance — why it matters

On broken rural roads, monsoon waterlogging, and big speed breakers, every mm counts. Below ~170 mm a loaded sedan starts scraping. Above ~190 mm you stop worrying.

A loaded car loses 15–25 mm of effective clearance. A sedan at 163 mm loaded behaves like ~140 mm.

4. The decision framework

Smooth-highway profile → lighter, cheaper, thriftier

If you mostly drive good city + highway roads, prioritise mileage, low price, 5-star safety, boot space. Sedans and light crossovers win: Dzire, Amaze, Fronx.

Rough-road / loaded profile → clearance, suspension, resale

If you do village roads, monsoon, ghats, or long loaded trips, prioritise ground clearance, SUV suspension, resale. Compact SUVs win: Brezza, Nexon, Victoris.

The single most useful question: what are the worst roads you'll drive regularly (or on your once-a-year trip)? Optimise for those, not for the highway you're on most days. The bad roads are where a wrong choice hurts.

6. Things every buyer should understand

What does "on-road price" actually include?

Ex-showroom is just the start. On-road = ex-showroom + insurance (1st year, recurs annually) + RTO road tax (~10–12% in Maharashtra, varies by state) + registration + FASTag + optional extended warranty and accessories. The RTO tax and insurance are the big jumps — budget ₹1.5–2 L over ex-showroom for a ₹8 L car.

What is the CSD scheme and can I use it?

CSD (Canteen Stores Department) is the defence forces' subsidised purchase scheme — serving and ex-servicemen get cars with part of the GST/cess waived, so ex-showroom is ~10–15% lower than the public price. If you're not eligible, your real price will be higher than the figures on this page; treat them as a relative comparison, not your quote.

Is the extended warranty worth it?

Standard warranty is usually 3 years / 1,00,000 km. The extended warranty (~₹16–21k) adds years 4–5. Worth it if you keep the car long-term — a single failure of an electronic module (infotainment, ESP/ABS, sensors) in year 4–5 can cost ₹25–60k, more than the warranty. Skip it only if you sell within 3 years. It covers mechanical/electrical breakdown, NOT wear items (brakes, clutch, tyres) or accident damage.

Mild-hybrid li-ion battery — should I worry?

Some Maruti petrols ("Smart Hybrid") add a small li-ion battery for idle stop-start and torque assist (better mileage). It's a minor failure-risk item out of warranty (~₹50–65k to replace), but rare and livable. The upside is ~2 km/l better economy. Not a dealbreaker, just a known line item.

Do seat covers affect side airbags?

Yes — if your car has seat-mounted side airbags (most 6-airbag cars do), the airbag deploys through a seam on the outer edge of the front seat. A cheap aftermarket cover that wraps that seam tightly can block deployment. Use the genuine/dealer cover, or insist on an "airbag-compatible" aftermarket cover with the tear-seam.

Hill start — do I need the handbrake on an incline?

Most modern cars have Hill Hold Assist (standard on many trims now). Stop on the incline, release the brake, and the car holds itself ~2 seconds while you move to the accelerator — no roll-back, no handbrake needed. The handbrake method still works and is a fine habit for very steep slopes, but it's optional when Hill Hold is fitted. The handbrake's real job is parking on slopes.

Why does resale value matter so much?

Over a 7–10 year keep, depreciation is often the single biggest cost — bigger than fuel or service. A car that holds 70% of value (Maruti) vs 40% (some others) can be ₹2–3 L cheaper to own despite a higher sticker. Resale is why a pricier-but-popular car can be the cheaper long-term choice.
